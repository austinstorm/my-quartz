> “I don’t think spirituality has ever really been analytically that useful,” Leigh told me straight up. “What you’re interested in is why people make the distinction. Yeah, that is your game.”
>
> And he’s right. That is the game.
>
> Because when Americans say “I’m spiritual but not religious,” they’re not making a neutral category distinction. They’re making a value judgment. Spirituality gets to be everything good—Leigh called it “the aspiring side, the mystical side” and I said “the ooey gooey, the warm stuff.” Individual experience. Authentic connection. Real seeking.
>
> Religion? That’s the bad stuff. Institutions. Hierarchies. Hypocrisy. The things people are fleeing.
