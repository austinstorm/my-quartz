> In his book The Master and His Emissary, Iain McGilchrist outlines the principle of art’s semi-transparency, stating that art’s central power is in letting us see through it:
>
> “We need to see through the eye, through the image, past the surface: there is a fatal tendency for the eye to replace the depth of reality—a depth which implies the vitality, the corporeality and the emphatic resonance of the world—with a planar re-presentation, that is, a picture. In doing so, the sublime becomes merely the picturesque.
>
> In art there needs to be a certain balance between the facticity of the medium and the something that is seen through the medium, what I have referred to in shorthand as semi-transparency” (373).
>
> McGilchrist draws the concept of semi-transparency from the Renaissance when the appreciation of the world’s beauty was rooted in viewing art (as well as scientific discovery) “as an indicator of something beyond” (312).
>
> Art, consequently, is meant to exist in a “between” state, the bridge for the reciprocal relationship between me and the Other (whatever is not “me”). For this reason it cannot stop at itself; art which does not direct the attention beyond itself bars our full engagement with it. It is the art which we feel lacks something, which we cannot be consumed by, of which we are too aware. Art that is too self-aware of its own nature, which references itself rather than something greater, fails to be art at all.

- Emi Delp [Why Christian Writing Isn’t Good](https://emidelpwrites.substack.com/p/why-christian-writing-isnt-good)
