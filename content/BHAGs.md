Big Hairy Audacious Goals, as articulated by James Collins and Jerry Porras in a 1996 Harvard Business Review article.

Good or bad? Mark A. Garcia suggests avoiding them:

> And most thoughtful Christians immediately realize what's happening when institutions do this in their self-promotion; it is in fact a reason institutions lose credibility in a world increasingly demanding “authenticity” and a functional coherence between what such institutions teach theologically and what they say and do in publicity and operations. 
>
> The problem is not necessarily with the content of these articulated BHAGs but with their confusion of Christian hopes with institutional goals. Christian hopes are driven by what God has said to and about his Church, none of which works with the metrics-focused approach of managerial enterprises. Our hope, not only as Christians but as those involved in institutions of theological education serving the Church, is spiritual (not visible), mysterious (not measurable), providential (not manipulatable), eschatological (not immediate or even this-lifetime oriented). Thus our hope focuses on the unique ways in which the Church alone (and not my institution) is the arena within which God has said he advances us in hope. For many reasons, some quite understandable, this is hard for seminaries and institutes to stomach, and even harder to embrace with joy.
