Overly incendiary title. The worst person you is, of course, *yourself*. Ala the Apostle Paul, but also Pogo: "We have met the enemy and he is us." But one of the best Internet memes of all time is, "The worst person you know just made a great point" (from ClickHole in 2018).

But I was thinking about this when the buzzword 'reformed' came up the other day and L had a visceral negative reaction. Where does this negative reaction come from? Twenty years ago, or maybe even as recently as ten years ago, the cage-stage conversionist was most likely to be a young man becoming reformed. They were looking for more rigor and depth than their local expression of Christianity afforded, loved cigars and C.S. Lewis... the pipeline was clear.

And they were kinda obnoxious.
## The Worst Person You Know has become ~~Reformed~~ Orthodox

But the reformed 'brand' seems to be waning. The elder statesmen are disappearing, and what leaders remain are more interested in their own cultural brand than in a broader 'reformed' resurgence.

Now, the rigor and depth people are questing for is not intellectual, but ritual and aesthetic.

The forerunner of this was pickup-artist Roosh V, who after a decade-long career became open to the "God pill" while high on shrooms. In 2019 he joined the Armenian Orthodox Church, and then found it insufficient and moved to the ROCOR.

### Orthodoxy carrying the Reformed torch

There are two points, here. One is that Orthodoxy now sustains a cultural energy that 'Reformed' used to carry. Whether the beauty is the point, as it may be for Josh, or the beauty is downstream of authority ('This is beautiful and it is the *correct* form of beauty because Apostolic authority says it is'). There is a personal rigor which is consistent with an 'anabolic' lifestyle - 'cigars are anabolic'. And there's a quest for the mind. Sub phronema and an appeal to the mystic for scholasticism and the systematic.

This is good news for 'Reformed', whatever that may mean, as what is meritorious in it will still be there waiting for current and future '*ressourcement*'.

It's hard to tell what this means for Orthodoxy. Many of the former "Young, Restless, and Reformed" guys are either deconstructed perennialists or are in more apostolic and liturgical churches. I imagine some of those currently converting to Orthodoxy will stay, and some will land somewhere else in the coming decades.
### Demographics

The second point is a hard one to wrap my head around.

The articles currently dominating the news cycle are about the rise of the apostolic churches - parish attendance rising for the first time in recent memory, record confirmations on Easter, that sort of thing.

There was a very spurious survey of voters that showed a significant uptick in Catholic vs. Evangelical identification.

I would celebrate growth in the Orthodox or Catholic church. But the data simply isn't there. There is no growth in the Catholic church without immigration (of both laity and priests) and retention of the 'next generation' is pretty grim:

https://reformedconformist.substack.com/p/a-look-at-roman-catholic-statistics

https://nineteensixty-four.blogspot.com/2026/03/talking-about-your-generation.html

The biggest trends in switching 'branches' of the Christian church are the same as they have been: from nominal Catholicism, Orthodoxy, or mainline denominational Protestantism, to parachurch ministries or vibrant evangelical / pentecostal / non-denom 'mall churches'.
