One person's testimony:

(I enjoy the 'it's not that deep' practicality of this)

> For now, I just want to answer the question: why didn’t we join an ACNA or Continuing Anglican Church?
>
> And the answer to this is really quite simple: Those aren’t in our community. The Episcopal Church is. It’s not all that deep. Of course, I do think there are interesting arguments for each side, but those didn’t really factor into the decision at a practical level. I think, if at all possible, people should aim to worship in their local community rather than driving 40 minutes past dozens of churches to find the “perfect” church. Others may disagree; that’s fine.



> Finally, I’ll not that Anglicanism, especially with it’s big tent nature, allows me to pursue ecumenism in a very natural way. I can see Christ in many institutions without any anxiety or difficulty. St. Dorotheos of Gaza once described Christian unity as like a circle with Christ at the center and our lives as radii. Unity didn’t come from us moving closer to other people, but all of us moving closer to Christ who is the center, to whom we are united by faith, and as we do that, we inevitably become closer to one another. So, at the end of the day, I desire Christian unity, but I think the means of achieving that is by having all of us draw close to Christ, whose body is the church. As a Protestant, I believe that body exists across multiple denominations which allows me to say, seek Christ, look for the preaching of the gospel and the administration of the sacraments, and be faithful to even the imperfect churches where you find Christ.
