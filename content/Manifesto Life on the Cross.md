"Ordo amoris" is Latin for the "order of loves". Developed by Augustine and Thomas Aquinas, it refers to the proper ordering of one's loves. 

“All men are to be loved equally,” Augustine wrote. “But since you cannot do good to all, you are to pay special regard to those who, by the accidents of time, or place, or circumstance, are brought into closer connection with you.”

We have a greater obligation to love our neighbors than we do to love someone on the other side of the world. This is often challenging. As P.J. O'Rourke says, "Everybody wants to save the Earth; nobody wants to help mom do the dishes."

But the teachings of Jesus complicate this. In answer to the question, "Who is my neighbor?" Jesus tells the parable of the Good Samaritan, expanding our obligation to love from people with whom we have a natural affinity to those who we would consider our natural enemies.
