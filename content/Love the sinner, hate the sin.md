A reasonable think on this old clam:


> In the Church Fathers, and in Orthodox Christianity more broadly, moral exhortation is therapeutic rather than forensic. Sin is not primarily a legal infraction that demands condemnation. It is a sickness that requires healing. The goal is not moral distancing but restoration.
>
> We often say, and rightly so, that the Church is a hospital.
>
> Hospitals do not shout diagnoses across the street. They do not announce cures from a distance. They draw the wounded inside, bind their wounds, and remain with them patiently as healing takes place. Ancient Christianity did not require slogans because it practiced medicine.
>
> If we are honest, “hate the sin, love the sinner” functions less like medicine and more like a courtroom verdict. It allows one to assert moral condemnation while simultaneously claiming compassion. It is a sentence well suited to social and political conflict, but poorly suited to repentance, confession, and healing.
