The right and the left tend to get folk culture wrong.

What forms of folk culture circulate most easily now, thanks to the internet?

Folk practices do not emerge from timeless rural idyll, nor do they function decoratively.

They were tools of cultural preservation in the face of pressure and social change. This bit is appealing to conservatives, who face a temptation to LARP. They were also about communities without power to organize themselves both socially and politically. This is appealing to liberals, whose lack of common culture makes it difficult to organize without succumbing to in-fighting.

Folk culture is about hope and agency and community in the face of the difficulty of life or opposition and oppression.

There's a growing interest in folk culture, but the most visible expression of this interest is on the Internet. Devoid of a strong sense of place or season, this interest is reduced to the nostalgic and the symbolic. A deracinated spectacle curated to appease the algorithm.

The algorithm explains how culture gets narrowed while simultaneously appearing to show us a much broader range of things that we would otherwise be exposed to. The spectacle explains what such a narrowed culture might look like.

	In *The Society of the Spectacle* (1967), Guy Debord used the term to describe a society in which lived experience is gradually replaced by representations of it. Social life becomes something people watch rather than do and culture is reduced to images and symbols. He wrote “The spectacle is not a collection of images, but a social relation among people, mediated by images”
	
	Debord’s concern wasn’t necessarily that meaning or history would disappear altogether, but that they would become detached from lived experience. As he put it, “everything that was directly lived has moved away into a representation.” In this sense, folk culture risks becoming something we no longer make together, but something already there which people just watch and share.

Nostalgia is a thing to look out for. Nostalgia itself isn't a problem, but when nostalgia is at the forefront it's a sign that the spectacle has taken hold. Nostalgia relies on things being 'recognizable' and emotionally charged.

In her 1977 essay collection *On Photography*, Susan Sontag wrote that “today everything exists to end in a photograph.” Now more than ever.

Is the point the appearance of folk culture, or the culture itself? The paradoxical pitfall is that the internet has trained us that the goal is accurate reproduction. The more accurately we reproduce, the further away from folk it becomes.

That is, you can reproduce the sign of the culture while being detached from the communal life that gives the culture its force. The antidote to this is reproducing things badly. This is the difference between *performing* a folk song and *covering* a song.

Real folk culture is not easily replicated. This is why pubs in the US are not the same as pubs in the UK. This also alleviates some concerns we may have about 'cultural appropriation'. When cultural practices are adopted in a living way by a people that didn't originate them, they are necessarily changed.

Folk cannot be exclusively individual. It has individual practitioners, of course, but it must have collective identity. Culture produces and arrives out of relationships between people, not out of images for people to consume.
