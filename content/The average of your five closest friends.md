Hustle culture has this thing wherein you're the average of your five closest friends. The lesson here is to choose your close friends wisely, because they represent in aggragate what you will become.

My perennial complaint about hustle culture is that it says too much and too little at the same time.

Is it possible that we are instead the 'average' of all of the people we encounter, our location, our experiences?

Jorge Luis Borges says "I am not sure that I exist, actually. I am all the writers that I have read, all the people that I have met, all the women that I have loved; all the cities I have visited."

Borges's quotation allows us to reframe this 'hustle culture' version of this concept as more self-emptying, and simultaneously generative. We are the product of everything, and we *give* to others of ourselves by knowing them. I have certainly hoped that everyone I know is weirder (in the best way) for knowing me, that their horizons are somehow broadened. I aspire to do this not by exerting influence over them, even if it's for their own benefit, but just by *being*.

"I have gathered a posy of other men’s flowers and only the tie that binds them together is my own." - Michel de Montaigne.

St. Francis's prayer:

O divine master grant that I may
not so much seek to be consoled as to console;
to be understood as to understand;
To be loved as to love
For it is in giving that we receive-
it is in pardoning that we are pardoned.
And it’s in dying that we are born to eternal life.
