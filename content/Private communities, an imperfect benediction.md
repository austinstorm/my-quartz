> The result is art and planning that travel well because they belong nowhere, the opposite of forms that feel inevitable to their materials and place. Only when means are re-ordered to an intelligible end—natural law disclosed in hill and wind, feast and bell—does culture become creative again rather than merely refined.
>
> This also explains why private communities can feel, however imperfectly, like a benediction: rules about scale, material, setback, canopy, and craft are a poor substitute for wisdom, but they are better than the entropy of emptiness. Think of a place like Barnsley resort, a beautiful shire-like cottages, gardens, golf, and wedding venue. Think of the American tourist who steps into a European street scaled to feet, where stone receives the light and shopkeepers keep holy the hour of supper, and suddenly feels—no, remembers—what flourishing is. The sensation is not sentimentality but true authentic instinct, we all know it’s true. It is the soul recalling that culture worthy of the name arises where rite and rule school desire, where tools take orders from ends, and where the human is permitted again to be a creature of form rather than a consumer of effects.
