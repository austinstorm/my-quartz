An accumulation:

1. "Our inability to 'represent' an emotional reaction to ourselves, to give it a name and a meaning, helps to lift us out of the immediacy of an experience so that we can respond to it effectively."
2. "Being mindful, having mindful awareness, is often defined as a way of intentionally paying attention to the present moment without being swept up by judgments."
3. "When we focus repeatedly on specific skills, moment-to-moment neural activity can gradually become an established trait through the power of neuroplasticity"

1. Life is a series of ongoing challenges. What perceptions do you hold that make this reality difficult for you?
2. Do you hold the perception that your problems are radically different than others? Explain your answer.
3. How often do you view life as others have is so much easier than you?
4. How often do you find yourself nervous from life running smoothly? Describe these times. Are they based in reality?

1. Consider and explain your resistance to asking for help - particularly asking others for help to assess reality and to develop thinking that's aligned with reality.
2. Consider your early life role models and how they were filled with lies and deception. Explain how this interferes with trusting that others will help you if you ask for help.
3. Consider what's involved in developing new ways of thinking about the role of trust. Explain what must be changed to help you trust more?

(these seem likely to be derived from Dan Siegel or Patrick Carnes)
