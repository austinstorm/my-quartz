> I think the more literate and more epistemically calm people are (and the less they have to lose), the more they can afford to be cosmopolitan, to entertain thoughts that aren't their own without being scandalized and threatened. Thus the post-cancelled scene on twitter, spanning the gamut from Red Scare to post rationalists to esotericists to ultra racist frogtwitter, come across as currently the most robust and ""reliable"" as a public network of information testing even though some hold views abhorrent to me as a Christian, or utterly incompatible with one another. Their commonality is that they don't get scandalized and block out opinions just because it's distressing to them. But there's a vanishingly small number of people capable of doing this, and willing to do it in public, and so when we want to engage with a broader set of society, we must necessarily become more factionalistic because we cannot appeal to cosmopolitan graciousness at scale.
