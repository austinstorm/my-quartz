> Finally, there is an incarnational seriousness about matter, an unusual reluctance to let the spiritual float free of bodies, places and things. Herbert keeps returning to the furniture of an actual church, Rossetti to the flesh-and-blood particulars of Revelation’s imagery; Temple went so far as to insist that Christianity is “the most avowedly materialist of all the great religions.” Even the shape of this list makes the argument—sermons, prayers, poems and devotions far more readily than systems, which suggests where Anglican theology has often actually lived: not chiefly in propositions, but in the embodied, communal practices those propositions were meant to serve.

- Mark Clavier [25 Essential Anglican Writers](https://markclavier.substack.com/p/25-essential-anglican-writers)
