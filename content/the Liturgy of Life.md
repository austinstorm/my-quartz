> But before the old wound enters the bloodstream of the race, there is not yet the modern fixation on epistemology. The nervous question of whether the Real is finally accessible, whether we are imprisoned behind appearances, whether meaning is only a symbolic projection. That anxiety is itself a symptom of exile. It is ontology before argument, the world given as intelligible gift, the creature placed within it to receive, to name, to tend, to guard, to return creation in thanksgiving. Adam does not stand over reality as a detached spectator; he stands within it as priest.


> This is the depth of a *liturgical-cultural ontology*. Before there is “culture” as we now catalogue it—before the long diaspora of rites and myths and nations—there is the creature placed in a world that is already gift, already sign, already charged with meaning, because it proceeds from God and remains oriented toward Him. Culture is a gift from God.


> I am Catholic in the blunt and absolute sense. I do not mean I admire the Church as a venerable institution, or find her rites psychologically sustaining, or prefer her aesthetic to the thin pieties of the age. I mean that I have bent the knee before the altar and confessed, without metaphor and without retreat, that the Eucharist is the flesh and blood of the risen Christ—God’s self-gift rendered in visible humility, the uncreated life offered under created signs. Eucharistic Realism.



> At last, we come to the condition that now presses upon every people, every household, every parish. It is the colonization of culture by technique. It’s important to name this precisely. Technique is not merely as technological machinery. It is an anti-culture—an anti-cultus—a rival liturgy that colonizes man and his peoples from the inside. Technique does not merely alter cultus; it quietly replaces their governing ontology. It gives them a new posture toward reality, the conviction that what is most real is what is most controllable, that the world is a problem to be solved, that time is a friction to be removed, that the body is a project, that limits are insults, and that the sacred is, at best, a private taste, a deep perennialism even.


> But what is needed is not a return to childhood enchantment, but a reacquaintance with the Real. A people must learn again the courage of limit, the dignity of form, the sacredness of time, the priestly meaning of labor, the discipline of desire, the joy of feast, the gravity of sacrifice. A culture is healed when its worship is healed—not because worship is one compartment among others, but because worship is the root from which all compartments receive their shape.
