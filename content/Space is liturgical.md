A great poetics of space, space as liturgical:

> Liturgy trains a people by the way a space breathes and a movement is made. Rooms, too, are teachers. The liberal shop—when it is good—stages a modest liturgy: warm light, measured proportion, honest material. You leave a little quieter inside, as if the place said grace on your behalf. Especially when the coffee is good, the music is light, and they have a nice leather chair you can sit in. 


He's super-duper wrong, at least in my context, about the central thesis: "It might be simple really, and this is because many conservatives just prefer black coffee1 because it fits the moral grammar they already live by, no frills, no foam, just the thing doing its job."

In my context the conservatives, who can be divided into blue-collar folks and non-denom pastor-type people, make coffeeshops where the coffee is good because a) hospitality / 'witness', or b) a craft approach to excellence. Like, the world has good music so our worship music has to be good, but applied to coffee.
