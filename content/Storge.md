In C.S. Lewis’s The Four Loves, he talks through the four classical types of love: storge, philia, eros, and caritas.

*Storge* is one of the trickier ones. It's like a particular fondness, but not necessarily in the sentimental sense.

Leah Libresco Sargeant says this:

> One way I think about storge if that it’s the kind of love you have for that one particular tree that sits three blocks from your house and marks the turn onto your street. It’s not that that tree is the tallest or most beautiful—it might not even be your favorite kind of tree in general—but it is your tree in a way no other tree is.
>
> And cultivating fondness and valuing it as a real kind of love is a good inoculation against [meritocratic or consummerized relationships].
